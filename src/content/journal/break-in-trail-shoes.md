Modern trail shoes need far less breaking in than the leather boots your parents wore. But a few short sessions still make the difference between a great first hike and a blister at mile three.

1. **Wear them at home for a few evenings.** Socks you'll actually hike in, laces tied the way you'll tie them.
2. **Take a short walk.** Thirty to sixty minutes, ideally with a hill. Notice any spot that rubs.
3. **Fix the hot spots before the long hike.** Re-lace, try a thinner or thicker sock, and tape anything that still rubs.

If a shoe still hurts after that, it's the wrong shoe, not a shoe that needs more breaking in. Bring it back and we'll find one that fits.
